01Quick start

SLAB/16 opens with a drum kit on the pads and a demo beat in sequence 1. You can make sound in the first ten seconds.

  1. Press Play or Space. Browsers only allow sound after you click or press a key, so the first press also switches audio on.

  2. Hit the pads along with the beat. With a keyboard, Z X C V are the bottom row of pads; the rows above are A–F, Q–R and 1–4. The keys sit in the same shape as the pads.

  3. Turn K1 to change the tempo. The three knobs always control the three values along the bottom of the display, whatever page you are on.

  4. Drag an audio file from your computer onto a pad. It replaces that pad's sound and is saved with your project.

  5. Press ? in the app at any time for the shortcut list, or use Tour at the top of the page for a guided two-minute walkthrough.

SHIFT works with one finger. Hold Shift and press another control, or tap Shift once and it stays lit for your next press. The second function of every button and pad is printed beneath it.

03Lessons

Seven short lessons, each one a real task. Work through them in order the first time.

Lesson 1 · Record your first beat

Start from an empty sequence so you can hear only what you play.

  1. Press Seq. The pads now choose between 16 sequences. Hit pad 3 to select empty sequence 03, then press Seq again to go back.

  2. Press Seq Record (Enter), then Play. You get a one-bar count-in with a click, then recording starts. Recording loops: each time round, add another part.

  3. First pass: kick (pad 1, Z) on beats 1 and 3. Second pass: snare (pad 3, C) on beats 2 and 4. Third pass: closed hat (pad 5, A) on every eighth note.

  4. Press Stop. Notes you played were snapped to the nearest 16th note. If a take went wrong, press Shift then − to undo it.

Fix it in step edit

Press Shift + Seq for step edit. The display shows a grid of the current bar for every pad in the bank; the pads become the 16 steps of the bar for the selected pad. Hit a pad to add or remove that step, or click straight on the grid.

STEP EDIT A01 Kick Deep · bar 1/2
K1 Pad 01 · K2 Bar 1/2 · K3 Velocity 100

Use K1 to choose which pad you're editing, K2 to move between bars, and K3 to set how hard new steps play. Lower velocities on in-between hats make a beat breathe.

Lesson 2 · Load and shape a sound

  1. Drag one or more audio files onto a pad. Several files fill that pad and the ones after it. WAV, MP3, AAC/M4A, OGG and FLAC all work, depending on your browser.

  2. Press Sample to edit the selected pad. On the Trim tab, drag the white markers on the waveform, or use K1 (start) and K2 (end), to cut off silence or keep one hit from a longer recording.

  3. Use the other tabs: Tune for pitch and level, Filter for a low-pass, high-pass or band-pass, Amp for fade-in, fade-out and stereo position.

  4. The soft buttons on the Trim tab reverse the sound, normalise it to full volume, or crop the file to your trim points.

Sample Select opens the sample pool: every sound in the project, including the factory kit. Turn K1 to audition and press Assign to put one on the selected pad.

Hats that cut each other off. On the Amp tab, give your open and closed hats the same Choke group. Hitting the closed hat now silences a ringing open hat, as on a real kit. The demo kit is already set up this way.

Lesson 3 · Sample from the mic, a tab, or yourself

  1. Press Sample Record. K1 chooses the source:

    • Mic: your microphone or audio interface. Your browser asks for permission the first time.
    • Tab audio: sound from another browser tab, such as a video or a record on a streaming site. When the browser asks what to share, pick the tab and turn on Share tab audio. This works best in Chrome and Edge on desktop.
    • Resample: SLAB/16's own output, including effects. Use it to print a pad-FX move or bounce a pattern into a single loop.

  2. Press Sample Record again to start, and once more to stop. Silence at the start of a mic or tab take is trimmed automatically. Takes stop at 60 seconds.

  3. Hit the pad where you want the take. Use Discard to throw it away instead.

Lesson 4 · Chop a break

Chopping cuts a loop into hits you can replay in a new order. It is the core sampling technique behind a lot of hip-hop and jungle.

  1. Load a drum loop onto a pad and select that pad. Trim it first if it has silence at either end.

  2. Press Chop. K1 picks the method: Equal cuts it into 2–16 equal slices (good for loops cut exactly on the beat). Transient cuts at each hit; raise K2 sensitivity to find quieter hits.

  3. The pads now play each slice, so you can check the cuts. K3 picks the bank the slices will go to.

  4. Press Apply. The slices land on the pads of that bank (pad bank shown on the top panel as A B C D). Play them in a new order and record the result.

To fit a loop to your tempo before or instead of chopping, use Warp (Shift + pad 15). Tell it how many beats the loop is: Stretch keeps the pitch, Repitch speeds it up or slows it down like a record.

Lesson 5 · Turn patterns into a song

  1. Build a few sequences: a main groove, a variation, a breakdown. Double on the Seq page copies a sequence to twice its length, giving you room to vary the second half.

  2. While the beat plays, select another sequence on the Seq page. It waits for the current loop to finish before switching, so changes always land in time.

  3. Open the song page with Shift + pad 12. K1 moves through the steps, K2 picks the sequence, K3 sets how many times it repeats. Add inserts the current sequence after the highlighted step.

  4. Turn on Song Mode and press Play to hear the arrangement from the top. It stops when the song ends.

  5. Press Bounce to render the song to a WAV file. It renders faster than real time and includes the filter, delay, reverb and compressor. Lo-fi and Pad FX are live only; resample them if you want them in the file.

Lesson 6 · Perform with effects

  • Pad FX turns the pads into 16 momentary effects on the whole mix. Hold a pad to apply one and let go to stop. Bottom row: stutters at ¼, ⅛, 1/16 and 1/32. Next row up: triplet stutter, roll, and two gates. Third row: filter sweeps, bit-crush and flange. Top row: half speed, tape stop, reverse and echo-out. Turn on Latch to keep an effect on until you hit the pad again.
  • Knob FX holds four effects that are always on: filter with drive, tempo-synced delay, reverb, and lo-fi. Each has a tab; K1–K3 shape it. Double-click a knob to reset it.
  • The fader is a DJ filter by default: pull it down for a muffled breakdown, push it up for a thin build, centre it to open.
  • Mute turns the pads into mute switches: drop the kick out for a bar, then bring it back.
  • Note Repeat: hold a pad and it retriggers on the grid set on the Time Correct page (Shift + pad 14). Shift + Note Repeat switches to triplets for rolls.

Pad FX and lo-fi are live only and aren't in a bounce. To keep a performance, Resample it (Lesson 3) onto a pad.

Lesson 7 · Play melodies on the keyboard

The keyboard is a second interface for the same instrument. Use the Sampler | Keyboard switch above it, or press `, to swap between the two. Both share your sounds, sequences and transport, so you can swap in the middle of a take and nothing stops.

  1. Switch to Keyboard. It plays whichever pad is selected, at any pitch. C4 is the sound's original pitch.

  2. Pick a sound in Sound (pad). Bank D holds the synth patches: Saw Lead, Square Bass, Warm Pad, Sub Bass and Organ, plus Keys and Chord Stab. Drums and your own samples work too.

  3. Play with the mouse or touch (slide across keys for a run, use several fingers for chords), a MIDI keyboard, or your computer keys: A S D F G H J K are white keys from the lowest C, W E T Y U are black keys, and Z / X shift the octave.

  4. To record a bassline or melody, press Rec and Play on the keyboard's strip, then play over the beat. Notes keep their pitch and length, and chords are recorded as chords.

  5. Switch back to the sampler to add drums, chop, or arrange. The notes you played are in the same sequence.

  • Sustain keeps notes sounding after you let go, until you turn it off. Handy for pads and chords on a touch screen.
  • Synth patches loop for as long as a key is held. Tone them on the sampler's Sample page: the Filter tab opens or darkens them, and the Amp tab sets fade-in and fade-out.
  • Load synth patch puts a patch on an empty pad (the selected one, or the first free pad in bank D) and selects it. It never replaces a sound unless every pad is full, and then it tells you.
  • To play a MIDI keyboard chromatically instead of triggering pads, set Notes Play to Keyboard on the MIDI page (Shift + pad 8).
  • Step edit shows when keyboard notes play, not their pitch. To change a note's pitch, undo and replay it.

04Drum patterns

Four starting points to enter in step edit. Each row is one pad from the factory kit, each column a 16th note, and each group of four is one beat. Dark squares are full hits; grey squares are soft hits (K3 velocity around 60–70).

To enter one: select an empty sequence, open step edit (Shift + Seq), set K1 to the pad in the first column, then hit the steps shown. Set the tempo and swing on the main page.

Habits that make beats sound finished

  • Vary velocity. Machine-even hats sound stiff. Turn off Full Level (Shift + pad 1) and play softer on in-between notes, or set lower velocities in step edit.
  • Add ghost notes. A very quiet snare or rimshot just before the main snare adds groove without clutter.
  • Leave space. If everything plays all the time, nothing stands out. Mute a part for a bar before a change.
  • Tune your drums. On the Tune tab, pitch the kick and 808 to the key of your melody. 16 Levels in tune mode (Shift + 16 Levels) plays one sound chromatically across all 16 pads; pad 5 is the original pitch.
  • Layer. Two kicks on different pads (a deep one and a punchy one) recorded on the same steps hit harder than either alone.
  • Watch the meter. Short flashes into amber are fine; red all the time means it's too loud. Lower pad levels rather than relying on the limiter.
  • Make variations, not new beats. Double a sequence, change the second half, and build the song from small differences.

Sampling responsibly

Sampling a commercial record for a released track usually needs permission from both the owner of the recording and the owner of the song. For music you plan to share or sell, use sounds you recorded yourself, royalty-free packs, public-domain recordings, or Creative Commons material whose licence allows your use. Most of the sound libraries in Resources show the licence on every file.

MIDI controllers

Plug in a USB MIDI keyboard or pad controller, open the MIDI page (Shift + pad 8) and press Connect. Your browser asks for permission once.

  • Notes Play chooses what notes do. Pads: notes from the base note upward (C2, note 36, by default) play pads 1–16 of the current bank. Keyboard: notes play the selected pad at pitch, like the on-screen keyboard. Most pad controllers send 36 from their first pad.
  • CC 1 (mod wheel), CC 74 and CC 71 turn K1, K2 and K3.
  • MIDI Start, Continue and Stop drive the transport, so a drum machine or DAW can start SLAB/16.

Web MIDI works in Chrome, Edge, Opera and Firefox. Safari doesn't support it.

07Saving and sharing

  • Autosave. Every change is saved in this browser within a second. Close the tab and it's all there next time, as long as you use the same browser on the same device and don't clear site data. Private windows forget everything when closed.
  • Project file. Project (Shift + pad 16) → Save File writes one .slab file containing your sequences, settings and every sample you loaded. Use it as a backup or to move to another computer. Open it with Open File, or drop it onto any pad.
  • Bounce. Bounce WAV renders the current sequence, or the whole song when Song Mode is on, to a stereo WAV you can share or finish in another app. On a phone, Share sends it straight to the share sheet.
  • Single samples. Shift + Sample Select saves the selected pad's sound, as trimmed, as a WAV file.
  • Install. In Chrome and Edge, Install App on the Project page (or the install icon in the address bar) adds SLAB/16 to your dock or home screen. It then opens in its own window and works offline. In Safari, use Share → Add to Home Screen or File → Add to Dock.

08Troubleshooting

I don't hear anything.
Click a pad or press Play: browsers keep audio off until you interact with the page. Check the volume knob on the top panel and your system volume. On an iPhone, turn off the silent switch; web audio follows it.
The meter moves but there's no sound.
Your browser is sending sound to a different output. Check which output device your system is using, or which one the browser is set to.
Playing feels late.
Bluetooth headphones and speakers add a noticeable delay. Use wired headphones or built-in speakers while recording. Closing other heavy tabs also helps on slower devices.
The microphone doesn't work.
The page must be served over HTTPS (or from localhost), and you must allow microphone access. If you blocked it, use the site settings icon in the address bar to allow it again. If no prompt ever appears, SLAB/16 is probably embedded in another page (such as a preview) that doesn't allow the microphone. Open it in its own tab. The record page tells you which of these it found.
Tab audio records silence.
In the browser's share dialog, choose a tab rather than a window or screen, and turn on Share tab audio. System-wide audio capture depends on your browser and operating system.
A file won't load.
Your browser can't decode that format. Convert it to WAV with a free editor such as Audacity and try again.
My MIDI controller isn't found.
Connect it before pressing Connect, and use Chrome, Edge or Firefox. If you denied permission, allow MIDI in the site settings and reload.
My project is gone.
Autosave is per browser and per device, and is erased when site data is cleared. Save a .slab file after any session you care about.
Pads keep playing after I let go.
The pad is set to loop. A looping pad keeps going until you hit it again. Turn off Loop for that pad, or press Stop to silence everything.

09Resources

Hand-picked places to learn and to find sounds. All free unless marked otherwise.

Learn music and production

  • Learning Musicinteractive

    Beats, notes, chords and song structure, taught hands-on in the browser. The best first stop if you're new.

    Playful experiments with rhythm, melody and harmony. Good for kids and for building intuition.

    Short lessons and drills for reading rhythm, scales, chords and ear training.

  • Hooktheorysome paid

    The chord progressions behind thousands of songs, for writing melodies and basslines over your beats.

  • Pocket OperationsPDF

    A free book of classic drum patterns in a 16-step grid, ready to enter in step edit.

    Drum patterns from electronic music styles, broken down step by step.

  • Sound On Soundsome paid

    Long-running magazine with in-depth technique articles on recording, sampling and mixing.

  • EDMProdsome paid

    Practical articles on arrangement, sound design and finishing tracks.

Find sounds to sample

  • FreesoundCC licences

    Hundreds of thousands of recordings uploaded by users. Each file shows its licence; filter for CC0 to use sounds without attribution.

    Free loops and one-shots from other producers, in many genres. Read the site's terms before releasing music with them.

  • 99Sounds

    Free, royalty-free sample packs: drums, textures and field recordings.

    Public-domain and freely licensed classical recordings and scores.
